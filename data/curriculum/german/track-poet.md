# Stage 5: The Poet

**Goethe, selections from *West-östlicher Divan* (fifteen poems, c. 2,000 words), plus 'Heidenröslein,' 'Erlkönig,' 'Der Zauberlehrling' (full).**

## Rationale

The crown of German lyric. The *Divan* poems are late Goethe, mature, Orientalist, compressed. The student finishes with the three most famous Goethe ballads memorized entire.

## Reading List

### From *West-östlicher Divan* (1819)

1. **Hegire** — "Nord und West und Süd zersplittern" — the opening poem. Goethe's escape to the East.
2. **Selige Sehnsucht** — "Sagt es niemand, nur den Weisen" — the moth and the flame. Transformation through death.
3. **Gingo biloba** — "Dieses Baums Blatt" — the ginkgo leaf sent to Marianne von Willemer. Unity in duality.
4. **Im Gegenwärtigen Vergangenes** — "Ros' und Lilie morgentaulich" — past love remembered in present beauty.
5. **Wiederfinden** — "Ist es möglich! Stern der Sterne" — reunion. The soul's rediscovery of itself.
6. **Phänomen** — "Wenn zu der Regenwand / Phöbus sich gattet" — the rainbow as symbol of love and art.
7. **Eins und Alles** — "Im Grenzenlosen sich zu finden" — the One and the All. Pantheism.
8. **Vermächtnis** — "Kein Wesen kann zu Nichts zerfallen" — his testament. What endures.
9. **An vollen Büschelzweigen** — roses and nightingales. The garden as erotic paradise.
10. **Nimmer will ich dich verlieren** — love as possession and surrender.
11. **Höchste Gunst** — "Dass wir solche Dinge lehren" — the poet's mission.
12. **Talismane** — "Gottes ist der Orient! / Gottes ist der Okzident!" — God owns East and West. Unity.
13. **Unbegrenzt** — "Dass du nicht enden kannst, das macht dich groß" — infinity as greatness.
14. **Wie mit innigstem Behagen** — pleasure in song and wine.
15. **In tausend Formen magst du dich verstecken** — God hidden in all forms. The divine in nature.

### The Three Ballads

16. **Heidenröslein** (1771) — "Sah ein Knab ein Röslein stehn" — the boy and the wild rose. Folk simplicity. Eight lines, three stanzas. You have read this already in Stage 3. Now memorize it.

17. **Erlkönig** (1782) — "Wer reitet so spät durch Nacht und Wind?" — the father, the son, the Erlking. Death and delusion. Thirty-two lines, eight stanzas. You have read this already in Stage 4. Now memorize it.

18. **Der Zauberlehrling** (1797) — "Hat der alte Hexenmeister" — the sorcerer's apprentice. The broom that will not stop. One hundred lines, fourteen stanzas. The longest poem in the course. Memorize the first four stanzas; read the rest with notes.

## Vocabulary

The *Divan* poems require a lexicon of c. 800 words. A glossed vocabulary list is provided. The three ballads require c. 300 words, overlapping heavily with the *Divan*.

## Grammar Notes

The *Divan* poems use the full range of German grammar: subjunctives, conditionals, relative clauses, genitive constructions, poetic inversions. The ballads are simpler, but *Erlkönig* uses indirect discourse and *Der Zauberlehrling* uses the imperative and modal verbs.

## Recitation

Memorize at least five of the *Divan* poems entire, plus the three ballads (or the first four stanzas of *Der Zauberlehrling*). Recite them aloud. German lyric is made to be spoken.

## Reading Pace

One poem every two days, with rereading. The entire track takes twelve weeks at a comfortable pace, eighteen if you memorize all eighteen poems.

## Capstone Preparation

The capstone examination for the Poet track will ask you to translate a quatrain from Goethe at sight (likely from the *Divan*), to parse ten inflected forms, and to compose five German sentences from English. You will also recite one memorized poem aloud (recorded or live).
