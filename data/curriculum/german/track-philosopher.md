# Stage 5: The Philosopher

**Nietzsche, *Also sprach Zarathustra*, Vorrede (Prologue), sections 1–10 (c. 8,000 words).**

## Rationale

Nietzsche at his most poetic and accessible. The mountain, the saint, the tightrope walker, the three metamorphoses. Prose that reads like verse. The most quotable German outside Goethe.

## Reading List

### *Also sprach Zarathustra* — Vorrede (1883)

1. **Zarathustra's Prologue, §1** — "Als Zarathustra dreißig Jahre alt war, verließ er seine Heimat und den See seiner Heimat und ging in das Gebirge." — Zarathustra leaves his home. Ten years in the mountains. The decision to descend.

2. **§2** — "Und also begann Zarathustras Untergang." — the going-down. The sun as teacher. "I must, like you, go under."

3. **§3** — The saint in the forest. "Könnte es denn möglich sein! Dieser alte Heilige hat in seinem Walde noch nichts davon gehört, dass Gott tot ist!" — God is dead, but the saint has not heard.

4. **§4** — Zarathustra arrives in the town. The crowd gathers for the tightrope walker. "Ich lehre euch den Übermenschen."

5. **§5** — The Übermensch. "Der Mensch ist etwas, das überwunden werden soll." — Man is something to be overcome. The speech to the crowd.

6. **§6** — The tightrope walker falls. The crowd scatters. "The rabble does not understand me."

7. **§7** — Zarathustra carries the dead tightrope walker. "Du hast aus der Gefahr deinen Beruf gemacht; daran ist nichts zu verachten." — you made danger your calling.

8. **§8** — Zarathustra buries the tightrope walker. "I will not speak again to the people. I will speak to companions."

9. **§9** — "Companions I seek, not corpses, nor herds, nor believers. Fellow creators I seek."

10. **§10** — "This is my morning; my day begins. Up now, up, you great noon!" — Zarathustra leaves the town. The Prologue ends.

## Vocabulary

Zarathustra's prose requires c. 1,200 words. A glossed vocabulary is provided. Nietzsche repeats his key terms: Übermensch, Untergang, Gefahr, Schaffende, Herde, Weisheit. Learn these first.

## Grammar Notes

Nietzsche writes in the Präteritum (narrative past) with frequent present-tense speeches. The subjunctive appears in conditional and optative constructions. Relative clauses are common. The syntax is mostly straightforward, but Nietzsche loves rhetorical inversions and parallelism. Read each sentence aloud to hear the rhythm.

## Style

Nietzsche is writing a parody of the Gospels and the Platonic dialogues. Zarathustra is an anti-Christ and an anti-Socrates. The style is Biblical: short sentences, parataxis, repetition, metaphor. "Also sprach Zarathustra" echoes "So spricht der Herr" (thus saith the Lord).

## Reading Pace

One section every three days, with rereading. The ten sections of the Vorrede take one month. Then read Part I (twenty-two speeches) over the next two months. The capstone examination covers only the Vorrede, but reading Part I will deepen your understanding.

## Capstone Preparation

The capstone examination for the Philosopher track will ask you to translate the opening of Zarathustra's Vorrede (§1, first paragraph: "Als Zarathustra dreißig Jahre alt war...") at sight, to parse ten inflected forms, and to compose five German sentences from English. You will also answer five short questions about the philosophical content of the Vorrede.

**Begin with §1. End with §10. In between, you will have heard the voice that shattered European philosophy.**
