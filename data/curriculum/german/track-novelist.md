# Stage 5: The Novelist

**Kafka, *Die Verwandlung* (entire — c. 22,000 words).**

## Rationale

The most famous opening sentence in twentieth-century literature. "Als Gregor Samsa eines Morgens aus unruhigen Träumen erwachte, fand er sich in seinem Bett zu einem ungeheueren Ungeziefer verwandelt." Kafka's German is modern, clear, unornamented. The student reads a complete novella in four to six weeks.

## Reading List

### *Die Verwandlung* (1915) — three parts

**Part I** (c. 7,500 words) — Gregor wakes as an insect. He cannot get out of bed. The chief clerk arrives. Gregor's family discovers his transformation. The chief clerk flees. Gregor's father drives him back into his room with a cane.

**Part II** (c. 7,500 words) — Gregor's sister Grete feeds him. The family adjusts. Grete and the mother try to remove the furniture from Gregor's room. Gregor clings to a picture on the wall. The father pelts him with apples. One embeds in his back.

**Part III** (c. 7,000 words) — Gregor weakens. The family takes in three lodgers. Grete plays the violin. Gregor emerges to listen. The lodgers see him and threaten to leave. Grete declares Gregor must go. Gregor retreats to his room and dies. The family goes on a tram ride. Spring arrives. Grete has grown into a young woman.

## Vocabulary

Kafka's vocabulary is modern and concrete. The novella requires c. 2,000 words, but 80% of the text is covered by the 1,000 most common German words. A glossed vocabulary is provided. Key terms: Ungeziefer (vermin), verwandeln (to transform), Zimmer (room), Tür (door), Familie (family), Arbeit (work).

## Grammar Notes

Kafka writes in the Präteritum (narrative past) with occasional present-tense dialogue. The subjunctive appears in conditional and indirect discourse. Relative clauses are frequent. The syntax is straightforward — Kafka is not an experimental stylist. He uses long sentences, but they are grammatically regular. V2 word order in main clauses, verb-final in subordinate clauses.

## Style

Kafka's style is flat, precise, bureaucratic. The fantastic premise (Gregor is an insect) is narrated without emotion or explanation. The effect is uncanny. The German is simple, but the simplicity is unsettling. Read each sentence slowly. Notice what Kafka does not say.

## Reading Pace

One part every ten days, with rereading. The entire novella takes one month at a comfortable pace, six weeks if you reread carefully. Some students prefer to read faster and then reread the whole text a second time.

## Annotations

The Kalopaideia edition provides notes on the thirty most difficult passages, plus a running vocabulary for words outside the 1,000-word core. You will not need a dictionary.

## Capstone Preparation

The capstone examination for the Novelist track will ask you to translate the opening sentence of *Die Verwandlung* ("Als Gregor Samsa eines Morgens...") at sight, to translate a second fifty-word passage from Part II or III with notes, to parse ten inflected forms, and to compose five German sentences from English. You will also answer five short questions about the plot and themes of the novella.

---

**Begin with Part I. End with Gregor's death and the tram ride. In between, you will have read one of the great works of European fiction.**
