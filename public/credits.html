Credits & Licensing

Kalopaideia is a publication of New Charter Ventures. It draws on a small number of openly licensed reference sources alongside its own original editorial work. This page records what comes from where, and under what terms.

Dictionary lookups (tap-to-define)

When you tap a word in a reader text, Kalopaideia first consults Wiktionary for an initial definition. The popover credits Wiktionary by name and links to the source article; where the definition has been adapted (shortened, stripped of formatting, or chased to a lemma), that is indicated as well.

Wiktionary content is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0) and the GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL). Any dictionary text in Kalopaideia that is reproduced or adapted from Wiktionary remains licensed under those same terms, and may be reused by anyone under either license.

Where Wiktionary is silent or incomplete, the popover falls back to an in-house lexicon entry generated by our editorial software. Those entries are credited as "Kalopaideia lexicon" and are © New Charter Ventures.

Daily word entries

The daily word card for each language — the headword, etymology, literary context, usage example, and "Did you know" note — is original editorial work composed by Kalopaideia's editorial team and software. It is © New Charter Ventures and is not derived from Wikipedia or Wiktionary.

The Akousma audio library

The works in The Akousma are public-domain texts of classical and medieval literature. The line-by-line audio narration, English translations (where marked as Kalopaideia editorial glosses), scholarly notes, and the line-segmentation are © New Charter Ventures.

Where a translation is reproduced from an out-of-copyright source, the translator and date are credited on the work’s landing page.

A note on the English we ship

Kalopaideia is, first, an audio reader of the original language. The Greek, Latin, Old English, German, French, Italian, Welsh, and Old Norse texts are the primary content. The English column is a secondary orientation aid; we are not selling a translation.

By editorial choice, we ship only translations that are in the United States public domain. That means Pope, Dryden, Chapman, Butler, Lang, Jowett, Longfellow, Cary, Gummere, Bowring, Wharton, Yonge, Cornish, Twain on Heine, Swinburne on Villon, Lady Charlotte Guest on the Mabinogion, Olive Bray on the Edda, R. K. Gordon on the Old English elegies, and other translators whose work was published before 1929 or who are otherwise demonstrably in the public domain.

We intentionally exclude the great modern translations — Lattimore, Fitzgerald, Mandelbaum, Fagles, Heaney, Wilson, Hammond, and their contemporaries — for two reasons. First, they are under copyright and cannot lawfully be used here. Second, we believe the line-by-line, somewhat archaic register of the Victorian and Edwardian translators sits well next to the original tongue; it pushes the reader to listen to the Greek or the Latin rather than read past it. Where no PD line-for-line translation exists for a passage we wish to include, we ship our own literal line-by-line gloss, labeled as a “Kalopaideia editorial gloss.”
