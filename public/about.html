What this is

Kalopaideia is a daily site of learning for the classical languages. Each morning, five words arrive: one from Latin, one from Ancient Greek, one from French, one from German, one from Old English. Each word carries its pronunciation, its inflection, its etymology, the literature it came from, and a short cultural vignette from the civilization that produced it.

The word

The Greek word paideía referred to the formation of the whole person through the study of poetry, rhetoric, philosophy, mathematics, and music. Not information; not utility. The shaping of judgment and taste. This site aspires to the small daily exercise that, repeated, becomes that formation.

Sources and scope

Words are drawn from the classical literary and philosophical corpus of each language. For Latin: Cicero, Virgil, Ovid, Horace, Tacitus, Seneca, Lucretius, Caesar, Catullus, Juvenal, and their peers. For Greek: Homer, Hesiod, Herodotus, Thucydides, Plato, Aristotle, the tragedians, the orators. For French: Molière, Racine, Hugo, Baudelaire, Flaubert, Zola, Proust, pre-1960. For German: Goethe, Schiller, Hölderlin, Rilke, and the philosophers Kant, Hegel, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche, Heidegger. For Old English: Beowulf, the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, the Exeter Book, Cynewulf, Caedmon, Alfred's translations.

Audio

Pronunciation is rendered through free text-to-speech using the closest available voice: Italian for Latin, Modern Greek for Ancient Greek, native voices for French and German, British English for Old English. These are approximations intended to give the sound of the word. For exact reconstructed classical pronunciation, consult a specialist.
